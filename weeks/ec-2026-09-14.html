Key takeaways

1
£1.50m is the lowest week in the window, and both segments fell. Existing −9.5%, new −10.9%. Orders −7%, basket −3%. 62% of brands down. Every day of the week below the day before. Sunday normalising from last week's £288k spike is 40% of the drop, so Mon–Sat like-for-like was −7.2% - still the weakest week since July.
2
Margin is at a window high on a shrinking base. Product margin 37.42% (+1.8pp) and retro-adjusted 39.74% are both the best in eight weeks, and this time retros halved so it is mix, not cover. The low-margin volume we lost - LMNT, Momentous, BON CHARGE, tech - is exactly what lifts the ratio. Good news that should not be read as the business improving.
3
Healf Week's two-week verdict: £94k of brand retro, no volume in either segment. NC fell 14% across the fortnight; existing fell 9% in week two after being flat in week one. Retro-adjusted margin rose 2.7pp. If the objective was margin it delivered; if acquisition, it did not. BON CHARGE is the clearest case - £28k with cover, £9.6k without, below where it began.
4
LMNT needs the 4for3 test checked before anyone reads it as demand. £69k is a window low, −55% since 20 Jul. The discount rate fell 2.8pp this week, which is what removing 4for3 would do. Anton confirmed the en-uk test is live and Harriet's 15 Sep thread was an LMNT customer whose 4for3 did not apply. If LMNT is in the test cohort this is an experiment cost, and one worth quantifying quickly.
5
Three unwinds explain £39.8k, but the book was still −7.5% without them. BON CHARGE's retro closing, Momentous's restock catch-up and Garmin's bounce are all mechanical. The remaining decline is broad and spread across wearables (−£30.5k across four brands, no price change) and mid-tier supplements.
6
Two things went the right way. Thorne held flat at £125.4k after two weeks of decline, with NC +8%. The Ultimate Human hit a window high at £25.1k with its retro cut 57% - the one brand that generated volume this week without buying it. Worth knowing what drove it.
