Key takeaways

1
First growth in four weeks, and it came without promo. £1.59m, +5.2%, on existing customers +8.2% with retros down 68%. Orders and basket both up. 320 of 559 brands rose - the broadest week since July, led by the core supplement book. Product margin before retros held at 37.08%; retro-adjusted 37.77% is the honest unpromoted baseline for October.
2
New customers are the problem, and it is getting worse. 6,037, the fourth consecutive fall - 7,449 → 7,068 → 6,403 → 6,037. Saturday's 757 was the lowest day in the window. 1–27 Sep has delivered roughly 25.7k against a 45k target; the month lands near 28.5k. Existing customers cannot carry Q4 on their own.
3
Garmin is back at its August level - and swinging with stock. NC £15.1k, all-customer £29.6k, the best week in the window, on a discount rate of 1.95% - people are paying full price. But it has gone £2.5k → £11.8k → £4.1k → £15.1k over five weeks, which is what intermittent stock looks like, not a demand trend. No launch or restock message in Slack this month. A lost revenue extract would settle it; if stock is the constraint, this is the easiest £10k a week on the page to make permanent.
4
Oura and WHOOP are both at window lows while the watch brands grow. Oura £58.6k → £26.2k in four weeks with cover held throughout; WHOOP £45.7k → £30.7k. Garmin, COROS and Withings all rose. That is brand-specific, not a wearables category swing. Both need an owner before the Q4 tech push.
5
Healf Week's durable return was BodyHealth and Energybits. Both grew after cover ended - BodyHealth to its best week in the window. Six others went back to base or below; BON CHARGE is 38% under its pre-retro level. £94k of cover, two lasting lifts.
6
Two corrections to last week. LMNT: 4for3 was never removed - the code is in all four weeks - and LMNT recovered +20% to £86.1k. One-week dip. The Ultimate Human: its growth carries a 33–42% discount rate, not "no price lever" as I said. It is now the eighth largest brand at £32.3k, mostly self-funded.
