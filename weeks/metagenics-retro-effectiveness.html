What the grid shows
Every week above £15k is a week we discounted. That relationship is consistent and it is the whole point — Metagenics responds to price. The question is who pays for it.

The three green June weeks and the two red weeks produced almost identical sales, £17.2k against £17.7k a week. But in June the rebate carried the discount and our margin only fell to 35.3%. In late July and August we ran the same play with no rebate and margin fell to 30.3%. Same volume, double the cost.

Note the third June cell. The retro ended on 29 June, so that week was only covered for one day — the rebate recovered just 2.1pp instead of 12.4pp, and the adjusted margin drops to 28.5%. When cover stops mid-week, we absorb the rest.
The April lesson — depth is not the driver, visibility is
April ran a 25% fully funded retro, deeper and better funded than June's 20% cofunded. It produced nothing: sales stayed at baseline (£11.8k, £11.7k, £12.9k), margin never moved from ~40%, and total rebate income was £62.

The reason is in the promo note — the discount code was gated behind the newsletter and not visible on the website. A better-funded, deeper discount that customers could not see did nothing. June's shallower, cofunded, visible discount drove +45%. Worth remembering when negotiating terms: a hidden 25% is worth less than a visible 20%.
