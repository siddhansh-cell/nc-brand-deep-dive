Sales fell 2.9% but the customer count fell 7.6%, and net sales per new customer rose to £68.31, the highest in the window. So basket is not the issue — acquisition volume is. That matters more than the sales line given the 45k NC quarterly target: we are converting fewer people at a better rate rather than losing spend.

The pattern across the window is consistent. Every week under 7,500 new customers — 13 Jul, 20 Jul and now 3 Aug — has a higher spend per head than the 8,000+ weeks. Volume weeks bring in lower-value customers, and quiet weeks skew to higher-intent buyers. Worth checking against CAC by week before drawing a conclusion, since a 7.6% volume drop at flat spend would be the cheaper explanation.

The headline −48.6% is measured against the retro peak, which is the wrong baseline — every retro falls from its peak. The question is where it settles, and Metagenics settled at £2,740 against a pre-retro base of £2,078. Now confirmed on a complete week rather than an extrapolation.

That makes it the counter-example the calendar needed. BON CHARGE is the contrast: pre-retro £5,497, and now £1,959 two weeks after support ended — it gave back the volume and more, helped by a comp discounting into the gap. Same mechanic, opposite outcome. The test is post-retro versus pre-retro, never post-retro versus peak. Worth understanding what Metagenics did differently and applying it across the calendar.

Seed — a whole brand switched off£1,859/wk → £0
The steadiest brand in the book, switched off by one SKU. DS-01 Daily Synbiotic ran £1,507–2,099 of NC every single week from June through 20 July with almost no variance — an £1,859/week average. Discontinued 28 July, £370 that week on residual stock, and £0 since. DS-01 was effectively the entire brand.

That is roughly £97k annualised from a single SKU decision. The tag is short-expiry rather than commercial, which matters — short expiry is a stock condition, not a decision to delist. Three conflicting accounts exist: compliance, short expiry, and an internal error since resolved. The brand is still at zero. Settle this first; it is the cleanest recovery available.
Organic Protein Company — starting to come back£112 → £776
OPC was accelerating when it was cut — NC ran £1,405 → £2,017 → £2,683 → £2,710 → £2,142 → £2,307 through June and July, averaging £2,461/week over its last four clean weeks. It collapsed to £112 and has now recovered to £776, so something is back on sale. Still £1,206/week below its 8-week average and £1,685 below its recent run-rate.

Durable growth is rare — Omnilux and Balance Phone are the only two we have identified — so a brand cut mid-ramp is worth revisiting. The partial recovery suggests it is retrievable.
Thorne — about half explained−£4,813 (−13.5%)

Against w/c 20 Jul, the last clean week, Thorne is down £4,821 and those two SKUs account for £2,354 — 49%. So roughly half the decline is catalogue and half is not yet explained. Worth a look across the rest of the range before assuming it is all discontinuations.

Key takeaways

1
The problem is acquisition volume, not basket. Sales down 2.9% but new customers down 7.6%, while net sales per new customer rose to £68.31 — the highest in the window. We are converting 627 fewer people at a better rate. Against the 45k quarterly NC target the count matters more than the sales line, so this is the number to watch. Worth pairing with CAC by week before concluding.
2
Tech held the week up. Oura (+£5.7k on the August retro), Garmin (+£5.1k), WHOOP (+£3.8k), Aarke (+£2.7k), Hyperice (+£2.7k) and Balance Phone (+£2.4k) added £20.5k between them. Oura's retro is the biggest single gain in the book. Without tech this week looks materially worse.
3
The Metagenics retro gain was real. It settled at £2,740 against a £2,078 pre-retro base — 32% above where it started — confirmed now on a complete week. BON CHARGE is the opposite: back below its pre-retro base with a comp discounting into the gap. Judge a retro on post versus pre, never post versus peak, and work out what Metagenics did differently.
4
Discontinued SKUs cost more than anything in the pricing queue. £6,342 of NC a week, ~£330k annualised, measured directly. Seed is at zero on a single short-expiry tag worth £1,859/week, with three conflicting explanations and no resolution. Organic Protein Company was cut mid-ramp and has started to recover, so it is retrievable. Add Inhale H2 at zero and there are three recoverable lines worth roughly £12k a week combined.
5
Back Omnilux and Balance Phone; stop reading debut weeks as run-rates. Omnilux is four weeks into four consecutive increases at £5,134 and still climbing in a down week. Balance Phone is up 33.6% into the top 10. Meanwhile Inhale H2, NIRA, Blueair, Nurosym, Lifepro, Reformed and 8HOURS gave back £14.9k between them after one strong week each. Two brands are compounding; the rest are noise.
