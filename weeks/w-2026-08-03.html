Metagenics — the retro gain was real

Metagenicsw/c 13 Julw/c 20 Jul (pre)w/c 27 Jul (retro)w/c 3 Aug (post, 6d)
Net sales£1,941£2,078£5,328£2,366
Per day (LFL)£277£297£761£394
vs pre-retro base——+156%+32.8%
Stabilised above where it started+32.8% vs pre-retro
The headline −48% is measured against the retro peak, which is the wrong baseline. The question is not whether Metagenics fell from its retro week — every retro does — but where it settled. It settled at £394/day against a pre-retro base of £297/day, so it is 33% above where it started and on a 7-day equivalent is running £2,760 against £2,078 before. The retro pulled the brand up to a new level and it has held there.

That makes Metagenics the counter-example the calendar needed. BON CHARGE ran a retro and settled at £271/day against a £785/day pre-retro base — it gave back the volume and then some. Metagenics did not. The lesson is that a retro can create a durable step up, and the test is post-retro versus pre-retro, never post-retro versus peak. Worth understanding what Metagenics did differently and applying it to the rest of the calendar.

New customer sales, so roughly a £70k/week NC run-rate removed from the catalogue. Tag split is 841 commercial, 31 short-expiry, 15 tag-removed, 9 generic. The practical use is diagnostic: when a brand declines, check this list before assuming demand or pricing.

One scope caveat before these figures go anywhere external. The per-SKU numbers are larger than the same brand's NC sales in this report. Seed DS-01 alone shows £7,177/week, but the entire Seed brand did £1,678/week in the eight weeks to 27 July on this report's basis. A single SKU cannot be four times its own brand, so the two extracts are on different scopes — most likely the SKU file spans all markets and channels while this report is UK e-com only, excluding Healf Zone, HX and POS. The direction and the ranking hold; the absolute £/week does not map one-for-one onto the tables above.
Thorne — decline now explained−14.2% LFL
Two high-velocity SKUs went on 28 July carrying £7.0k/week between them — Magnesium Glycinate at £5,355 and Whey Protein Isolate Chocolate at £1,643. Both are tagged short-expiry rather than commercial. The timing fits: they were still selling residual stock through w/c 27 Jul, which is why that week looked flat, then ran dry in w/c 3 Aug. This is a supply and catalogue decision showing up a week late, not a demand problem, and it should not be read as Thorne weakening.
Seed — a whole brand switched off£2,092 → £370 → £0
Seed had been one of the steadiest brands in the book, running £1,500–2,500 of NC every week for at least three months with no drift. DS-01 Daily Synbiotic was effectively the whole brand. It was discontinued on 28 July and Seed has now recorded two consecutive weeks at or near zero. On its prior run-rate that is roughly £2k of NC a week, or £100k annualised, from a single SKU decision.

The tag is short-expiry, not commercial, which matters: short-expiry is a stock condition rather than a decision to delist. There are three different accounts of why this happened — compliance, short expiry, and an internal error since resolved — and the brand is still at zero. Worth settling, because if it was an error or a temporary expiry issue this is the single cleanest recovery available.
Organic Protein Company — cut while it was compounding£2,307 → £112 → £728
This is the one that stings. OPC was not a flat brand being tidied up — it had more than doubled over eight weeks, £1,219 → £1,451 → £1,496 → £1,097 → £1,405 → £2,017 → £2,683 → £2,710, one of the few genuinely compounding brands in the catalogue. SKUs across its range were then discontinued in July and NC fell to £112 before a partial recovery to £728.

Given how rare durable growth is — Omnilux and Balance Phone are the only two we have called out — losing a brand mid-ramp is worth understanding. If the discontinuation was commercial rather than supply-driven, it is worth revisiting on the strength of that trajectory.
Truvani and Vital Proteins
Truvani took the hit as expected — NC £1,134 → £136 → £174 → £27 → £14, effectively gone from a steady £1–2k a week. Vital Proteins has not shown a clear NC impact, moving £542 → £1,034 → £341, which is noise at that volume — it had already declined from £1,836 back in May, so the discontinued SKUs were likely already low-velocity on NC.

Key takeaways

1
Read −5.0%, not −18.5%. The raw number is a six-day week against seven. But −5.0% like-for-like is a real decline, and the breadth confirms it — 213 brands up against 276 down, an almost exact mirror of last week. The run-rate is back below the June average after one week above it.
2
The Metagenics retro gain was real. It has settled at £394/day against a £297/day pre-retro base — 33% above where it started — and held there for a full week after support ended. The −48% headline measures against the retro peak, which is the wrong baseline. Compare post-retro to pre-retro, never to peak. BON CHARGE is the contrast: it settled well below its pre-retro base. Retros can create a durable step up, and Metagenics is the one to learn from.
3
Balance Phone and Omnilux are the two new brands actually picking up. Omnilux has gone four weeks, four increases, to £4,888 and is still climbing in a down week. Balance Phone is holding its restock gain at £7,304, up 18.2%. Both are compounding rather than spiking. Everything else that debuted strongly has given it straight back — Inhale H2 to zero, and NIRA, Blueair, Reformed, Solawave and 8HOURS all down 60–90%. Back the two that are building.
4
Tech is holding the week up. WHOOP (+12.1%), Oura (+17.7% on the August retro), Aarke (+15.2%), Balance Phone (+18.2%) and Hyperice (+33.8%) are all up. Without them the week would look considerably worse than −5%. Oura's August retro is the clearest thing working right now.
5
Discontinued SKUs are quietly costing more than any pricing issue. 897 SKUs since 1 July, £70k/week of NC run-rate. Seed is at zero two weeks running after losing its only meaningful SKU, worth ~£2k of NC a week, with three conflicting explanations and no resolution. Organic Protein Company was cut while it was compounding — it had doubled in eight weeks. Alongside Inhale H2 sitting at zero, that is three recoverable lines. Settle Seed first; it is the cleanest.
