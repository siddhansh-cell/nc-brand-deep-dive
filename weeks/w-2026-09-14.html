NC Brand Sales Deep Dive

−10.9% WoW

w/c 7 Sep vs 14 Sep 2026 — New customer net sales by brand (UK e-com, excl. Healf Zone, HX, POS). Healf Week closed on 18 Sep. NC £443.5k, new customers 6,407 - both the lowest in the window. Retros fell 43% as brand windows closed, yet NC product margin rose 3.3pp to 33.79%. We are selling less, to fewer people, at a higher margin.

Healf Week did not deliver volume. Across its two weeks NC went £513.2k → £497.6k → £443.5k and new customers 7,449 → 7,068 → 6,407. Retro-adjusted NC margin went the other way, 32.02% → 34.35% → 36.23%. BON CHARGE is the case study: £28.2k with £12.3k of cover, £9.6k the week it stopped - below where it started. The cover bought sales for exactly as long as it ran.

Key takeaways

1
Lowest NC week and lowest new-customer count in the window. £443.5k and 6,407, below even w/c 10 Aug. Every day was below the same day the week before. Three weeks of September have delivered roughly 20.5k new customers against a 45k target - the run rate needs to lift 60% in the final ten days.
2
Healf Week was a margin event, not a volume event, and now that is settled. Across its two weeks NC fell 14% and new customers fell 14%, while retro-adjusted NC margin rose 4.2pp to 36.23%. £94k of brand retro was collected. If the aim was acquisition, it did not work; if the aim was margin, it worked well. The brief for the next one should say which.
3
BON CHARGE shows the retro bought sales only while it ran. £7.8k → £11.3k → £28.2k → £9.6k. The week after the window it is below where it started. Metagenics took £8.7k of cover and fell 22.5% in week two. Set against June's Metagenics (+45%, held), the pattern is: retros lift a brand that is under-trading; they do nothing for a brand already at its ceiling. The Steph message needs that caveat.
4
Wearables fell together for the first time. COROS −£7.7k, Garmin −£7.6k, WHOOP −£4.8k, Oura −£3.7k - £23.9k and 44% of the NC decline. No retro or discount change on any of them. Worth checking paid tech spend and placement this week before reading it as a demand shift.
5
LMNT needs the 4for3 test checked. NC £7,769 and all-customer £69.0k are both window lows, −55% since 20 Jul. This week the discount rate fell 2.8pp. Anton's en-uk 4for3 removal test went live around 15 Sep and LMNT was the brand in Harriet's complaint thread. If LMNT is in the test cohort, this is a controlled experiment reading, not a demand problem - and a fairly expensive one.
6
The Ultimate Human is the one brand that grew through it. NC +123% to £15,864, all-customer +37% to a window high, with retro cover cut 57%. Something other than price drove this. Worth finding out what, because it is the only lever this week that produced volume.
7
Garmin was a bounce, not a recovery. I called it resolved last week. Units 55 then 32, discount doubled to 9.3%, NC back to £4.1k. Stock landing released a week of pent-up demand; what happens next depends on whether stock is short again. Lumie and Organic Protein Company both show first signs of recovery. A lost revenue extract would settle all three.
