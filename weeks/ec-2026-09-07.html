Key takeaways

1
Healf Week week one was a margin week, not a volume week. Sales flat at £1.67m, orders −2.0%. Retros quadrupled to £59.9k against only £7.1k of extra discount, so retro-adjusted margin rose 2.04pp to 39.06%, a clear 2pp above anything else in the window. The brands funded the week. The customers did not turn up in greater numbers.
2
The retro landed on existing customers. 69% of it, £41.2k. Existing-customer sales rose 0.5%, new-customer sales fell 2.9% and new customers fell 5.1% to 7,070. That protects margin on repeat purchases of retro'd brands, which is worth having - but it is not acquisition. If the aim of Healf Week is new customers, week one has not delivered them.
3
Sunday rescued the week. Mon–Sat was −3.2% like-for-like; Sunday 13 Sep at £288.5k was the best day of the fortnight and pulled it back to −0.5%. Friday and Saturday were the two weakest days for the second week running. Worth knowing what drove Sunday before reading it as momentum.
4
Two stock recoveries are £18.1k of gains, and two are still to come. Garmin (+£9.5k) and Momentous (+£8.6k) both returned to their pre-OOS level, confirming last week's lost revenue read. Lumie (−43%, third week down) and Organic Protein Company (£1.4k, seventh week near zero) have not. Those two were losing £11.8k a week to availability.
5
Thorne and LMNT are both at window lows. Thorne £124.8k, −20% since 27 Jul. LMNT £80.4k, −48% since 20 Jul. Neither is discounting less and neither is stock-constrained. They are the first and third largest brands in the business and between them are −£19.3k this week. These two need owners before Q4.
6
The retro thesis held, with exceptions. Seven of nine brands that gained cover grew; four of six that lost cover fell. BON CHARGE doubled twice in two weeks and broke into the top 10 at £28.2k. Wild Nutrition took £3.3k of cover and fell 15% - check the discount was visible. Metagenics is flat in week one against a base that had already stepped up.
7
Balance Phone has broken its ramp. Seven weeks of growth to £26.5k, then −51% to £10.9k. Units halved across all three variants, Kids hardest. Discount went to zero. The slide started 3 Sep and is not a stock-out. Back-to-school closing is the likeliest read but is not confirmed - worth a question to Sacha on placement and paid.
