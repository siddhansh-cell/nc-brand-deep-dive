Pricing fix — where the margin is leaking

NC margin improved +0.24pp this week, but none of it came from pricing. Mix and cost did all the work; pricing pulled the other way. That is the headline for this week: we are growing margin in spite of our price positions, not because of them.

Retro spend actually fell week on week (£6,943 → £5,563) yet still cost -0.32pp, meaning the drag is structural rather than campaign-driven. Split by domain, the leak has a clear home: EN-UK carries a -0.67pp pricing drag against -0.10pp on .com. EN-UK margin went backwards overall (34.52% → 34.44%) while .com added +1.57pp.

Second pricing workstream: subscription vs one-time price

A separate checker audited 343 subscription → one-time mappings across 627 products and returned multiple failures where the subscription price is not at least 10% below the one-time price. Blueprint Longevity Mix came back at 5.0% and several Humantra flavours at 5.0% or 0.0%. Where subscription offers no real saving, we are paying the subscription margin cost without buying the retention benefit.

What to fix
  • Clear the RRP alert backlog. 15+ SKUs sitting 10-41% below RRP. Each one is both a margin leak and a brand-relationship risk of the kind that nearly cost us matching permission on BioCare in March.
  • Treat EN-UK as the priority domain. -0.67pp pricing drag against -0.10pp on .com. The RRP alerts are all en-uk prices, so the two problems are the same problem.
  • Pure Encapsulations first. Largest single drag at -0.08pp on completely flat sales (+£3 WoW) — we are giving away margin and buying no volume with it.
  • Close the subscription price gaps. Either restore a genuine 10%+ subscription saving or stop discounting those lines.
  • Re-test the SURI assumption. SURI is still priced below RRP yet sales halved. If price were the driver, volume would have held. Worth understanding what actually drove the two-week spike before we credit pricing for it.

Key takeaways

1
Pricing was the only negative line in the NC margin bridge. The +0.24pp improvement came entirely from mix (+0.28pp) and cost (+0.24pp); pricing took -0.27pp back out. We are improving margin around our price positions rather than through them, and retro spend fell week on week yet still cost -0.32pp — so the drag is structural, not campaign-driven.
2
EN-UK is where the leak sits. -0.67pp pricing drag against -0.10pp on .com, and EN-UK margin went backwards (34.52% → 34.44%) while .com added +1.57pp. Every SKU on the 24-25 Jul RRP alert list is an en-uk price below brand RRP, 15+ of them between 10% and 41% under. Clearing that backlog is the single highest-value pricing action available this week.
3
SURI breaks the pricing narrative we have been running. It halved (-57.5%) while still priced 10% below RRP and with zero lost revenue — no price change, no stock gap. We credited three weeks of growth to its pricing advantage; this week says something else was driving it. Worth resolving before we use SURI as evidence for price-led growth anywhere else.
4
Pure Encapsulations is the cleanest available fix. Largest single pricing drag at -0.08pp on sales that moved £3 week on week. We are giving away margin and buying no volume with it. Green People (-0.03pp) is the same problem in reverse — we cannot match, so we are losing the sale and the season, with two summer SKUs flagged in July still unresolved.
5
The recovery is real but partial. +3.6% to £505k after last week's drop, with 249 brands up against 241 down, but still ~5% below the £531k June run-rate. Lost revenue of £109k across 197 brands remains the largest single drag, and Oura Ring alone accounts for £22k of it — a fifth of the total from one brand.
