Key takeaways

1
The recovery is done, and stock did it. £536.8k is the best NC week since 22 June and marginally ahead of the June average. OOS lost revenue fell 22% to £84.7k, and the two biggest resolutions — Oura Ring and Balance Phone — are the two biggest sales recoveries. 286 brands up against 213 down is the widest positive spread in the window, so it is broad as well as traceable.
2
Metagenics is the retro to learn from. +156% to £5,328, straight into the top 20, and 52% above its previous best. Every other retro this month — BON CHARGE, Garmin, Therabody, Hyperice — handed the volume straight back the week after. Metagenics stepped up to a new base instead. Worth understanding why and applying it to the rest of the calendar.
3
New brands are landing well. Inhale H2 debuted at £4.1k, the strongest opening week in the window. Omnilux held and grew again to £4.0k in week three, so it has found a level rather than spiking. Balance Phone nearly doubled to £7.2k back in stock. Together these three added £8.3k this week.
4
Retro payback is now a reliable pattern. Four brands in four weeks — Therabody, Hyperice, BON CHARGE and now Garmin — have returned to roughly their pre-retro level within a week of support ending. The true incremental on these is well below the in-retro uplift, which is worth building into how we plan and value the calendar.
5
Two pricing problems and two new stock risks. H2Tab (−22.5%) broke a three-week run on comp discounting with the match request still open, and nama (−25.5%) remains structurally uncompetitive against its own D2C site. On stock, Lifepro jumped to £3.5k of lost revenue on 2 SKUs and Withings to £1.9k on one — both concentrated enough to fix quickly. Everything else in the decliner list is retro timing, a one-off order, a deliberate delisting or a model change. COROS has stabilised and Aarke posted its best week in the window.
