Metagenics+£2,011 (+96.7%)Retro
The retro delivered, and it delivered on a six-day week. Metagenics nearly doubled to £4,089, its best week in the window and comfortably above the £3,496 it did in early July. Worth pushing harder — this is the profile you want more of in the retro calendar.

Key takeaways

1
Metagenics is the retro to learn from. +96.7% on a six-day week, roughly +130% like-for-like, and it finished above its pre-dip level rather than spiking and fading. Set that against BON CHARGE, Therabody and Hyperice, which all handed the volume straight back. Worth understanding what made this one different and applying it to the rest of the retro calendar.
2
Stock coming back is doing real work. Balance Phone returned to stock and posted its best week since launch, +84% on a short week, with Pro and Kids both healthy. HigherDOSE and Momentous are also holding their restock gains rather than spiking. Availability, not demand, has been the binding constraint on these three.
3
Price keeps setting the direction. SURI recovered as soon as we matched again, H2Tab lost a three-week run the week a comp discounted, Maurten fell back once the launch discount was pulled, and Kiki is steady with resellers cut off. Four of the biggest swings are pricing decisions, and three remain open in #discounting-issues.
4
A third of the decline is not real. Bala's one-off unwinding, the FÔLD / Fittle / CELLER8 high-ticket swings and Seed's compliance discontinuation together account for roughly £12k of decliner value with no trading signal in any of it. Stripping those out, and allowing for the missing day, the week is a continued recovery.
5
COROS needs a decision, not another week of watching. Four straight weeks down, £22.7k to £12.7k, and the largest genuine decline in the book. MAP blocks any price response while comps keep running codes. The open question is whether we lower the pricing threshold and accept the margin trade-off. Every other major decliner is explained by retro timing, order lumpiness, a known pricing flag or a deliberate delisting. COROS is not.
