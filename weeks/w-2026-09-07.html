−2.9% WoW

w/c 31 Aug vs 7 Sep 2026 — New customer net sales by brand (UK e-com, excl. Healf Zone, HX, POS). First week of Healf Week (7–18 Sep). NC £498.1k, new customers −5.1%. Retros quadrupled to £59.9k and retro-adjusted margin hit 39.06%, the best in the window by 2pp, but volume has not responded yet. Garmin is back.

Healf Week, week one. A wave of brand-funded retros switched on from 7 September - Metagenics, Niagen, Wild Nutrition, Arrae, BodyHealth, Promix and a dozen more, on top of BON CHARGE's 4–13 Sep window. Total retros went from £14.6k to £59.9k. Retro-adjusted product margin rose 2.04pp to 39.06%, the highest in the window by a clear 2pp. Sales were flat and new customers fell 5.1%. The brands paid for the discount; the volume has not arrived yet. Second week will tell.

Key takeaways

1
Healf Week week one: the brands paid, the volume has not arrived. Retros quadrupled to £59.9k and retro-adjusted margin hit 39.06%, the best in the window by 2pp. But NC fell 2.9% and new customers fell 5.1% to 7,070, the second lowest week since July. Sunday 13 Sep at £288.5k was the strongest day of the fortnight - without it the week was −4.2% NC. Week two decides whether this was a margin win or a missed volume opportunity.
2
Oura and Balance Phone are the whole decline. −£24.8k between them against a total NC fall of −£15.1k, so the rest of the book grew £9.7k net. Oura is falling back from a two-week record as its retro cover was cut 18%. Balance Phone is more worrying: seven weeks of ramp to £19.6k, then halved with discount pulled to zero. The slide started 3 Sep and is not stock. Worth a question to Sacha.
3
Garmin is resolved, and it was stock all along. NC £2,529 → £11,763, back to its mid-August range on 55 units with the discount rate down to 4.4%. Last week's report named six SKUs at zero inventory carrying £4,736 of lost revenue; this week proves the diagnosis. Three reports of "cause unexplained" were an availability problem.
4
The retro mechanic held, with more exceptions. Seven of nine brands that gained cover grew, four of six that lost cover fell. Wild Nutrition took £3.3k of cover and fell 15%; Withings lost cover and rose 65%. BON CHARGE has doubled two weeks running as cover went £0 → £3.2k → £12.3k, and its window closed 13 Sep - next week shows what holds.
5
Metagenics shows no lift in week one of its retro. Units +17%, discount rate up 2pp, £5,391 of cover - the mechanic is visibly live. But sales are +3.0% against a base that had already stepped up to £16–17k in late August. June's +45% was measured against £11.9k. If week two does not move, the "do more of this" message to Steph needs a caveat about base effects.
6
Thorne is drifting and nobody is looking. £155.8k on 27 Jul to £124.8k now, −20% across the window and the lowest week in eight. NC −15% this week on units −8% at a flat discount. It is the largest brand in the business. LMNT is the same story one size down: £80.4k, also a window low, −48% from 20 Jul.
7
Stock: two of last week's five are back, two are still out. Garmin and Momentous recovered. Lumie (−43%, third week down) and Organic Protein Company (£99 NC, seventh week near zero) have not. Those two were losing £11.8k a week to availability. No lost revenue extract this week, so a refreshed one would confirm what is still at zero.
8
The early Q4 signal paused. Hatch, Lumie, Caraway, Black+Blum and Loop all gave back last week's gains; only Bearaby and Lola Blankets rose. Two of the five were stock-constrained. The advent calendar took £5,338 on 20 units in its first week, almost all existing customers.
